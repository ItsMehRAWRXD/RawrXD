## I/O-overlap hypothesis (evidence-bounded; not a promotion claim)
1. At 8GiB-class budgets, wall is mostly storage: Rust/C measure ~78–83% I/O.
   Perfect hide of I/O behind compute ⇒ theoretical residual ≈ compute-only ≈ 17–22% of wall
   ⇒ order-of-magnitude upper bound ~4–6× if moves always finish before consumers need them.
2. kimi trunk already implements FIXED-order L→L+1 overlap (k3_trunk_prefetch):
   measured laptop: 71.75 → 42.27 s/token ≈ 1.70× (k3_trunk.h commentary).
   That is the empirical proof that async next-consumer move removes a LARGE fraction of stall
   even WITHOUT more RAM — when the next consumer is known.
3. Remaining ladder gap 26.5 → 5.6 s/token (~4.7×) is mostly PINNING (fewer bytes read),
   not arithmetic. Expert path is data-dependent (896 top-16); LRU helps but is not determinism.
4. Deep2 can remove MOST of the *serial* stall when:
   - FutureConsumer nextUse drives UnifiedAsyncMove before Current compute
   - FreeToken dual-slot ring holds Current + Next (recycle Past)
   - HOST_DECODE path uses host-side async read (not only Vulkan PrefetchAsync)
   - ProductOpenSession stops requiring full tensor residency (stream like kimi trunk)
5. Deep2 cannot claim kimi's 1.70× or 4–6× on RawrXD GGUF until measured under purified runner.
   This gate seals the DISSECTION TARGETS, not a TPS promote.
