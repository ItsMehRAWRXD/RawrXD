STATEMENT
  While mapping memory architectures, readback windows, and host↔device
  synchronization against the live baseline, the architectural premise
  that the host must perpetually retrieve, validate, and route intermediate
  execution state was exposed as a legacy paradigm — not a law of nature.

  The prior campaign optimized the cost of crossing the boundary, treating
  transit as necessary and benign (PATH_A: maps, invalidation, coherency).

  The observation: that boundary is not benign necessity. It is an active
  violation of computational gravity. Device-resident state generated at
  high velocity has weight and momentum. The cycle
    force across narrow bus → stall device → wake slower host for routing
    → push instruction back
  disrupts that momentum. Persistent maps, cache invalidations, and sync
  barriers are life-support for that philosophy — measured improvements
  under GENERATION_WALL_NS do not prove the boundary is structurally required
  when the next true consumer is already on-device.

  When the full lifecycle of a state transition (inception→consumption)
  remains inside its native high-bandwidth topology, dual-domain coherency
  scaffolding ceases to be load-bearing. The host role shrinks from
  micro-manager to prime mover: establish boundary conditions, then step away.

  Removing that baseline is conceptual purification — abandoning the illusion
  that host-level observation is required for execution — not merely deleting
  syntax. "Benign" bridges become parasitic when they feed a device consumer.
